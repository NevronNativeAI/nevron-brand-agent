**Use these to orient, not to copy.** Almost every slide has its own layout —
that is deliberate. Read them to absorb how far the composition can move while
staying recognisably the same deck, then compose fresh for the argument at hand.
A new deck that reproduces these eighteen shapes in order has missed the point.

Note how much of this deck is *not* abstract — modules, services, touchpoints,
agents. Those slides belong to a product deck that happens to share the styling.
For an abstract deck, take the temperament and the first half's moves; leave the
feature grids alone.

## The arc

Read as an argument rather than a set of slides:

1. **Re-align** — why we are in the room (01–02)
2. **The problem**, made concrete and a little uncomfortable (03)
3. **The idea**, named (04)
4. **How it is built**, at the level of concept not feature (05–08)
5. **What it produces** (09–13)
6. **Why now** — the forces making this the moment (15)
7. **Where it sits** and who already trusts it (16–17)
8. **The ask** (18)

An abstract deck usually needs 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8, and much less of 4 and 5. Ten
to fourteen slides is the normal length. Eighteen is a long meeting.

## Imagery

Every hero is a generated 3D render, never a photograph: a luminous torus, a
glass cube with a letterform inside, an orbital sphere. They share one lighting
setup — near-black ground, one blue key light, volumetric haze, photoreal
material, nothing else in frame.

They are also composed for the slide: the subject sits in one half and the other
half falls to near-black so type has somewhere to live. That is why the image
prompts in this skill describe placement before subject.

Do not reuse the images from this deck in a new one. They belong to this
argument, and reusing them makes two decks look like the same meeting.
